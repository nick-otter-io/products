Currencies

Play each group in Listen2Page. The heading is what you should hear. One unit is singular. Any other amount is plural. A rate with a slash also lives in Units, and it is repeated here because the dollar sign is the point.

$1 is one dollar. Any other dollar amount is dollars. $0.50 is fifty cents.

The toll is $1.

The book costs $5.

The total is $5.00.

Lunch was $12.50.

The stamp is $0.50.

The penny is 1¢.

The fare is 99¢.

A $5 bill is torn.

Save $5 on the second copy.

A comma groups thousands. Million and billion stay those words, with dollars.

The grant is $1,200.

The grant is $1,200,000.

The round was $3.2 million.

The deal is $1.5 billion.

The round was $2.5B.

Revenue was $800M.

A currency code is the currency. The symbol and the code are the same money.

Pay USD 20.

Pay 20 USD.

The price is US$20.

The fare is C$20.

The hat is A$15.

The invoice is EUR 25.

Send GBP 10.

£1 is one pound. €1 is one euro. ¥500 is five hundred yen. ₹80 is eighty rupees.

The fare is £1.

The fare is £10.

The fare is £10.50.

The ticket is €1.

The ticket is €25.

The snack is ¥500.

The fare is ₹80.

Words that already say the currency stay words.

The price is five dollars.

The fare is ten pounds.

A slash after a price is per, and the unit is the full word.

Rent is $10/lb.

Pay is $15/hr.

Spice is $4/oz.

A dash between prices is to.

Tickets are $10-$20.

The estimate is $1,200 to $1,500.

A leading minus, or an amount in parentheses, is minus.

The balance is -$40.

The adjustment is ($40).
